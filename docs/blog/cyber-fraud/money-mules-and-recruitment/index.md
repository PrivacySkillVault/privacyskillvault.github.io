# Money Mules and Recruiters: How Fraud Proceeds Move Through Other People

<small>CYBER FRAUD · FINANCIAL CRIME EXPLAINER · 13 NOV 2026 · 8 MIN READ</small>

<p class="psv-article-breadcrumb"><a href="../">← Cyber Fraud</a><span> / </span><a href="../../">Cyber Journal</a></p>

A money mule is a person who transfers or moves money on behalf of someone else, often to help criminals disguise the origin or destination of illicit proceeds. Criminals recruit mules through fake jobs, romance relationships, social media, messaging platforms or direct approaches.

Some people know they are helping a criminal operation. Others may believe they are performing legitimate work or helping someone they trust.

## Common recruitment approaches

- A remote job offers payment for receiving and forwarding funds.
- An online romantic contact asks to use the victim's bank account.
- A supposed business partner asks someone to process payments.
- A recruiter promises commission for opening accounts or moving cryptocurrency.
- A person is asked to receive funds and withdraw cash or buy gift cards.

A request to use a personal bank account to process money for an unknown third party is a serious warning sign.

## Why mule networks matter

Mule accounts can create distance between the original victim and the people organising a fraud. Funds may pass through multiple accounts, payment providers, cash withdrawals or virtual-asset services. Each hop can complicate tracing and recovery.

## Awareness and involvement

The FBI describes unwitting, witting and complicit money mules. These labels describe different levels of awareness and participation. An investigator should not assume intent solely from account activity; knowledge and circumstances require evidence.

## Red flags for individuals

- You are asked to receive money and forward most of it elsewhere.
- You may keep a commission for moving funds.
- The job has no clear business purpose or verifiable employer.
- You are asked to open accounts for another person.
- A romantic contact asks you to receive or transfer funds.

Do not continue moving suspicious funds or attempt to return money to a sender using details provided in a suspicious message. Contact your bank through its official channel to understand how to secure the account and handle the funds.

## Investigator's perspective

Map incoming and outgoing transactions, timestamps, counterparties, account ownership, device and session evidence, cash withdrawals and related communications. Distinguish the account holder, recruiter, beneficiary and suspected organiser. A transaction pattern can support a hypothesis but does not by itself establish that the account holder knowingly participated.

## Current threat context

FATF's November 2026 report on scam compounds describes criminal networks using mule accounts, cash, payment services and virtual assets to move proceeds. It also emphasises that some people forced to work in scam operations are trafficking victims. Investigators should distinguish account activity, knowledge, coercion and intent rather than treating every intermediary as a willing participant.

## Key takeaways

Money mules are used to move illicit proceeds and obscure financial trails. Recruitment may be deceptive or deliberate. Preserve evidence, avoid unsupported assumptions about intent and report suspected mule activity through appropriate channels.
