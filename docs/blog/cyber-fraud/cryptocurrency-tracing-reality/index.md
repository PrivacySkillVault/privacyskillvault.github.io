# Cryptocurrency Tracing: What Blockchain Analysis Can—and Cannot—Reveal

Public blockchains can provide a persistent record of transactions between addresses. This transparency can help investigators follow the movement of certain cryptoassets. However, tracing an address is not the same as identifying the person controlling it.

## What on-chain analysis can show

Depending on the blockchain and available data, analysts may examine transaction hashes, sending and receiving addresses, amounts, timestamps, token contracts, transaction paths and interactions with known services.

Blockchain analytics may identify clusters of addresses that appear to be associated with the same service or entity. Such clustering is an analytical conclusion and should be supported by a transparent methodology.

## What it cannot prove by itself

- A wallet address does not inherently contain a person's legal identity.
- A transaction path does not automatically identify the beneficial owner.
- A service label or cluster attribution may be incomplete or mistaken.
- Movement through a mixer, bridge or exchange can complicate analysis.
- An on-chain transfer does not by itself establish the real-world purpose of a payment.

## Why off-chain evidence matters

Attribution may require exchange or virtual-asset service provider records, customer due diligence information, account records, device evidence, communications, IP logs and lawful investigative requests. The quality and availability of these records vary by provider and jurisdiction.

## A defensible tracing workflow

1. Preserve the original transaction hash and network.
2. Verify the asset, contract and chain.
3. Reconstruct relevant transaction paths without assuming ownership.
4. Record the analytics provider, labels and methodology used.
5. Identify points where funds interact with a regulated or identifiable service.
6. Seek corroborating off-chain evidence through authorised processes.
7. Document uncertainty, alternative explanations and the time of analysis.

## Be cautious of recovery scams

After a crypto loss, victims may be contacted by people claiming they can recover funds for an upfront fee. Verify any service independently. FATF has warned about fraudsters impersonating official bodies and offering fake asset-recovery services.

## Key takeaways

Blockchain analysis can reveal transaction flows, not automatically real-world identities. Good attribution combines on-chain evidence with reliable off-chain information and documented methodology.
