# Caller ID Spoofing Explained: Why the Number on Your Screen Can Mislead You

<small>CYBER FRAUD · TECHNICAL EXPLAINER · 13 NOV 2026 · 7 MIN READ</small>

<p class="psv-article-breadcrumb"><a href="../">← Cyber Fraud</a><span> / </span><a href="../../">Cyber Journal</a></p>

Caller ID spoofing occurs when caller-identification information is altered so that a call appears to come from a different number. A criminal may make a call appear local, imitate a known organisation or display a number associated with a victim's bank.

The displayed number is a presentation of calling information; it is not strong proof of the caller's identity.

## Why criminals use it

A familiar number can make a recipient more likely to answer and trust the caller. Spoofing may support bank impersonation, government impersonation, technical-support scams or other social-engineering attempts.

Spoofing does not necessarily mean the criminal controls the phone number being displayed. It also does not prove that the legitimate subscriber's device or account has been compromised.

## Caller ID versus authentication

Caller ID helps a recipient decide whether to answer. Authentication establishes identity through a trusted process. A phone number displayed during an incoming call should not replace independent verification, especially when the caller asks for money, credentials or a security code.

## How to respond

1. Treat unexpected requests for money or security codes as suspicious.
2. End the call rather than using a number supplied by the caller.
3. Contact the organisation using a number from its official website, app or physical card.
4. Report repeated spoofing to your telecommunications provider and relevant regulator.
5. Preserve call details if the call is connected to a financial loss or threat.

## What investigators can and cannot infer

A screenshot of caller ID may establish what appeared on the recipient's device. It does not by itself establish the true originating caller, physical location or person responsible. Investigators may need carrier records, call-routing information, platform records and corroborating evidence, obtained through appropriate legal processes.

## Key takeaways

- Caller ID can be spoofed.
- A displayed bank number does not authenticate an incoming caller.
- The legitimate subscriber may have no involvement in the spoofed call.
- Independent callback and verification are safer than trusting the display.
