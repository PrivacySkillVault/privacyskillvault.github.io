# Refund Fraud and Fake Tracking ID (FTID) Schemes: Understanding the Risk

<small>CYBER FRAUD · FINANCIAL CRIME EXPLAINER · 13 NOV 2026 · 8 MIN READ</small>

<p class="psv-article-breadcrumb"><a href="../">← Cyber Fraud</a><span> / </span><a href="../../">Cyber Journal</a></p>

Refund fraud covers dishonest attempts to obtain money or goods through a retailer's refund, return or dispute process. One term used in online discussions is **FTID**, commonly expanded as **Fake Tracking ID**. It is associated with schemes in which a return label or tracking information is manipulated so that tracking appears to show a return shipment reaching a retailer, even though the expected merchandise is not returned. The U.S. Department of Justice has described FTID fraud in a criminal case involving altered return labels. This is a specialist retail-fraud typology, not a general name for every tracking discrepancy.

Terminology and practices vary. FTID is not a universal legal category, and a tracking discrepancy alone does not prove fraud.

## How return and refund abuse can occur

Retailers may face dishonest claims involving non-returned goods, substituted items, false non-delivery reports, altered documentation, account misuse or manipulation of tracking information. Some cases involve organised groups; others may be isolated disputes.

## Why the distinction matters

A legitimate parcel can be delayed, misrouted, scanned incorrectly or lost. A customer may also have a genuine problem with a return. A retailer should not classify a claim as fraudulent solely because tracking data is unusual.

## Evidence to review

- Original order and payment records.
- Return authorisation and customer communications.
- Carrier acceptance and delivery scans.
- Package weight and dimensions, where available.
- Warehouse intake and inspection records.
- Refund approval, timing and payment settlement.
- Repeated patterns across accounts, addresses or devices, assessed under privacy and fairness controls.

Correlate independent records and retain original carrier data. Do not treat an address, device or household shared by multiple customers as conclusive evidence of common control.

## Prevention for merchants

Use consistent return policies, secure refund approvals, carrier integrations, exception review and proportionate account-risk controls. Ensure staff can escalate unusual cases and legitimate customers have a fair appeal route.

## If you are a customer

Keep proof of postage, tracking receipts, photos of the parcel and retailer correspondence. Contact the retailer and carrier through official channels if a return is disputed.

## Key takeaways

- FTID is a specialist term associated with false or manipulated tracking in refund abuse.
- Tracking anomalies require investigation, not automatic accusation.
- Retailers should combine carrier, warehouse, payment and account evidence.
- Fair dispute handling protects both merchants and legitimate customers.
