# What to Do After a Cyber Fraud Incident: A Practical Damage-Control Guide

<small>CYBER FRAUD · INCIDENT RESPONSE GUIDE · 13 NOV 2026 · 9 MIN READ</small>

<p class="psv-article-breadcrumb"><a href="../">← Cyber Fraud</a><span> / </span><a href="../../">Cyber Journal</a></p>

If you suspect cyber fraud, act quickly but use trusted channels. The first priority is to limit further loss and secure affected accounts. Preserve evidence where possible; do not delay urgent contact with your bank or service provider to collect screenshots.

## Immediate actions

### 1. Contact the financial institution

Use the number on your card, official website or banking app. Report the suspected fraud, identify affected transactions and ask whether payments can be stopped, recalled or disputed. Request a case reference.

### 2. Secure affected accounts

From a trusted device, change compromised passwords, revoke active sessions and review recovery methods. Secure the primary email account because it may control password resets for other services.

### 3. Contact the mobile provider if the number is affected

If your phone suddenly loses service or you suspect a SIM swap, contact the carrier through an official channel and ask it to secure the number.

### 4. Preserve evidence

Keep original emails, messages, call logs, transaction references, wallet addresses, receipts and relevant timestamps. Do not edit originals. Record what happened in chronological order.

### 5. Check devices

If malware or remote access is suspected, stop using the affected device for sensitive activity and seek trusted technical support. Avoid installing random cleanup tools or paying an unsolicited recovery service.

### 6. Report the incident

Report to the relevant police, national cybercrime reporting service, financial regulator or consumer-protection authority in your jurisdiction. Procedures differ by country.

## What not to do

- Do not send more money to someone promising guaranteed recovery.
- Do not share OTPs, passwords or recovery codes with callers.
- Do not delete messages or wipe a device before considering evidence preservation.
- Do not confront a suspected criminal or attempt to recover funds yourself.
- Do not assume a payment can always be reversed.

## A short incident record

Write down when you first noticed the issue, how contact occurred, what information was shared, which accounts or devices were involved, transaction details, who you contacted and the case references received.

## Key takeaways

Speed matters, especially for payment recovery and account containment. Contact providers through official channels, secure accounts, preserve evidence and report through the appropriate local process.
