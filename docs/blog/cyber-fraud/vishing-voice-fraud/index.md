# Vishing: Understanding Voice Calls Used in Cyber Fraud

<small>CYBER FRAUD · ATTACK METHOD · 13 NOV 2026 · 7 MIN READ</small>

<p class="psv-article-breadcrumb"><a href="../">← Cyber Fraud</a><span> / </span><a href="../../">Cyber Journal</a></p>

Vishing, or voice phishing, uses phone calls, voice messages or internet-based calling to deceive a target. A caller may pretend to represent a bank, police service, government department, employer, delivery company or technical-support provider.

The objective may be to obtain credentials, persuade a customer to disclose a one-time code, make a payment, install remote-access software or reveal information for a later attack.

## Common vishing scenarios

- A fake fraud department claims an account is under attack and asks the customer to move funds.
- A caller impersonates technical support and requests remote access.
- A fake official threatens legal consequences unless a payment is made.
- A criminal pretends to be a family member or colleague in an emergency.
- A caller asks the customer to read an OTP or approve a notification.

Voice cloning and AI-generated audio can make impersonation more convincing, but a familiar-sounding voice is not proof of identity.

## Caller ID is not authentication

Caller ID can be manipulated or spoofed. The number displayed on a handset may not reliably identify the person or organisation initiating the call. A call that appears to come from a bank should still be independently verified.

## Safe response

- Do not share passwords, PINs, OTPs or recovery codes with an incoming caller.
- Do not transfer money to a so-called safe account because a caller instructs you to.
- End the call and contact the organisation using the number on its official website, card or app.
- For a family emergency, call the person back using a known number and verify with another trusted contact.
- Do not install remote-access applications at the request of an unsolicited caller.

## If money or access has already been lost

Contact the bank or payment provider immediately using an official channel. Ask about stopping or recalling the payment, securing the account and revoking sessions. Preserve call logs, messages, payment references and application details.

## Investigator's perspective

Record the claimed organisation, displayed number, call time, duration, requested action and any codes or approvals the victim says they shared. A displayed number alone is weak attribution evidence. Correlate it with provider records and other evidence where legally available.

## Regional context

Callers may impersonate banks, government services, delivery providers or employers in both India and Saudi Arabia. A caller may know personal details and still be fraudulent; such details can be obtained from many sources. Verify through an independently obtained official channel, not a callback number dictated by the caller.

## Key takeaways

Vishing relies on conversation and pressure. Caller ID and voice familiarity are not proof. Pause, end the incoming call and independently verify before taking action.
