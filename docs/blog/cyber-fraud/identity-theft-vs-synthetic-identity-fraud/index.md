# Identity Theft vs. Synthetic Identity Fraud: What Is the Difference?

<small>CYBER FRAUD · IDENTITY FRAUD EXPLAINER · 13 NOV 2026 · 8 MIN READ</small>

<p class="psv-article-breadcrumb"><a href="../">← Cyber Fraud</a><span> / </span><a href="../../">Cyber Journal</a></p>

Identity-related fraud can involve a real person's information, a fabricated identity, or a combination of genuine and invented details. The distinction matters because detection, customer impact and investigation steps can differ.

## Identity theft

Identity theft generally involves the unauthorised use of another person's personal information to impersonate them or commit fraud. Information may include names, dates of birth, identification numbers, addresses, account credentials or other identifiers. An offender might use the information to access an existing account, apply for a product or impersonate the victim to a service provider.

## Synthetic identity fraud

Synthetic identity fraud involves creating an identity from a combination of genuine, fabricated or manipulated information. A real identifier may be combined with an invented name or date of birth. The resulting profile may not correspond to one real person. Synthetic identities can be used to open accounts, build an apparently credible history and later obtain credit or services fraudulently.

## Key differences

| Area | Identity theft | Synthetic identity fraud |
|---|---|---|
| Identity basis | Often impersonates a real person | Combines real and fabricated attributes |
| Victim impact | A real individual may see records misused | A real person may be linked through one exposed attribute, or no single direct victim may be apparent |
| Detection | Unauthorised account activity or victim report | Inconsistent attributes, unusual account relationships or lifecycle patterns |
| Investigation | Establish the genuine person's identity and unauthorised use | Establish which attributes are genuine, fabricated or reused |

Real cases may combine both forms.

## What investigators should examine

Review identity verification results, document provenance, contact details, device and session history, account-opening events, address and phone reuse, application patterns and links between accounts. A shared attribute is a lead, not proof that two applications belong to the same person.

Do not assume an identity is synthetic simply because a record is incomplete or unusual. Data quality, legitimate name variations and system integration issues can create false positives.

## Protective steps

Use identity verification appropriate to the service and risk. Protect personal information, monitor account-opening notifications, secure email and phone accounts, and report suspected misuse promptly to the relevant institution and local authorities.

## Key takeaways

- Identity theft usually misuses information belonging to a real person.
- Synthetic identity fraud combines genuine and fabricated attributes.
- One matching identifier does not establish common ownership or criminal intent.
- Document the provenance and confidence of every identity attribute.
