# Personal Digital Privacy Audit: A Practical Review of Your Online Footprint

A personal privacy audit is a structured review of the information you share, the accounts that hold it and the services that can access it. The NIST Privacy Framework is primarily an organisational risk-management framework; it should not be treated as a ready-made consumer checklist. The goal is not to disappear from the internet; it is to understand exposure and reduce unnecessary access.

## 1. Review your accounts

List important accounts such as email, banking, cloud storage, social media, shopping and government services. Remove accounts you no longer use where possible. Update recovery methods and enable strong authentication.

## 2. Review app permissions

Check which applications can access location, contacts, microphone, camera, photos and Bluetooth. Remove permissions that are not needed for the app's core function. Review background access and tracking settings.

## 3. Check public information

Review social-media profiles, public posts, old usernames and exposed contact details. Avoid publishing travel plans, identity documents, boarding passes or sensitive account information.

## 4. Review connected services

Check third-party app access to email, cloud storage and social accounts. Revoke integrations you no longer recognise or use. Be careful with browser extensions and apps that request broad permissions.

## 5. Secure recovery channels

Your primary email account can reset many other accounts. Secure it with a unique password, strong MFA and current recovery details. Protect your mobile-provider account against unauthorised SIM or number transfers.

## 6. Understand data exposure

A privacy audit cannot guarantee that personal information has never been copied or breached. If you find evidence of identity misuse, secure the affected accounts and contact the relevant service through a verified channel.

## Regional identity-data caution

In India, avoid sharing full Aadhaar, PAN or identity-document images unless the service and purpose are verified and the disclosure is necessary. In Saudi Arabia, apply the same minimisation to national identity and Iqama-related information. A document image or identifier can be reused in social engineering, account recovery attempts or impersonation. Never post identity documents, boarding passes or QR codes publicly.

## A repeatable checklist

- Review account sessions and devices.
- Remove unused applications and permissions.
- Check social-media privacy settings.
- Revoke unused third-party access.
- Update passwords and MFA on critical accounts.
- Review cloud-sharing links and backup settings.

## Key takeaways

Privacy is an ongoing process. Reduce unnecessary data sharing, limit permissions, secure recovery channels and review access periodically.
