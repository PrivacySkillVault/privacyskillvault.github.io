# Impersonation Fraud: Business Email Compromise and Romance Scams

<small>CYBER FRAUD · ATTACK METHOD · 13 NOV 2026 · 9 MIN READ</small>

<p class="psv-article-breadcrumb"><a href="../">← Cyber Fraud</a><span> / </span><a href="../../">Cyber Journal</a></p>

Impersonation fraud occurs when a criminal pretends to be a trusted person or organisation to influence a victim. Two important examples are business email compromise (BEC) and romance scams. They look different, but both exploit trust and can lead to financial loss.

## Business email compromise

BEC commonly involves an attacker impersonating an executive, employee, supplier or business partner. The request may ask an accounts-payable team to change supplier bank details, make an urgent transfer or share sensitive information.

A compromised mailbox is one possible route, but not every BEC attempt requires account compromise. Lookalike domains, spoofed sender information and unrelated accounts can also be used.

**Defensive controls:** verify bank-detail changes using a previously known phone number, require dual approval, protect email accounts with strong authentication, monitor mailbox forwarding rules and escalate unusual payment instructions.

## Romance scams

Romance scams involve a criminal creating a false romantic or personal relationship, often through dating platforms or social media. Over time, the criminal may request money for an emergency, travel, medical costs, business problems or an investment opportunity.

A common warning sign is a person the victim has never met in person asking for money, financial access or help receiving and forwarding funds. Some romance scams evolve into investment or cryptocurrency scams.

**Defensive controls:** be cautious when an online contact avoids independent verification, asks for secrecy or introduces financial opportunities. Never allow someone you have not independently verified to use your bank account.

## What the schemes share

Both can involve a credible identity, plausible story, gradual trust-building, urgency and a request to move money. Criminals may use genuine personal or business information obtained from public sources or prior compromise.

## Investigation approach

For BEC, preserve original email headers, mailbox audit logs, payment instructions, beneficiary changes, approval records and supplier verification history. For romance scams, preserve platform messages, account identifiers, payment receipts, wallet addresses and the relationship timeline. Avoid asking victims to delete conversations or confront the suspected criminal.

## Key takeaways

- Impersonation does not always mean the real account was hacked.
- Verify payment changes through a known independent channel.
- A familiar writing style does not authenticate an identity.
- Contact the bank promptly if a transfer was made.
