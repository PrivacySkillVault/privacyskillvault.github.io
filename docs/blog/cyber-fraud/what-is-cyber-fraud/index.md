# What Is Cyber Fraud? Understanding the Digital Fraud Ecosystem

Cyber fraud is the use of digital systems, communications or services to deceive people or organisations for financial or other unlawful gain. It may involve a fake message, compromised account, manipulated payment, misuse of personal information or abuse of a legitimate online service.

Cyber fraud is not one technique. It is an outcome that can be produced by several methods, sometimes combined in one incident.

## Cyber fraud, cybercrime and cybersecurity

**Cybercrime** is a broad term for unlawful activity involving computers, networks or digital technology. **Cyber fraud** focuses on deception or dishonest conduct intended to obtain money, property, credentials, services or another benefit. **Cybersecurity** is the practice of protecting systems, information and services from threats, including fraud.

A phishing message may be the initial technique; account takeover may be the resulting compromise; an unauthorised transfer may be the financial impact. Describing each stage separately makes an investigation clearer.

## The ecosystem

A fraud ecosystem can include a target, an initial contact channel, an enabler such as stolen credentials or malware, access to an account or payment process, movement of proceeds, and people or services that help monetise the activity. Not every case contains every role. Some fraud is committed by one person; other schemes are distributed across jurisdictions.

## Common categories

Examples include phishing, impersonation scams, account takeover, payment redirection, investment fraud, romance scams, identity theft, card-not-present fraud, business email compromise and refund abuse. These labels describe different aspects of activity and are not mutually exclusive.

## How to think about a fraud event

Separate the **method**, **compromise**, **transaction** and **impact**. For example, a customer may be persuaded by a fake bank call to disclose an OTP, after which an attacker adds a beneficiary and initiates a transfer. The call is the social-engineering method; disclosure is an enabling event; account access and payment are subsequent events; the loss is the impact.

## What organisations should do

Use layered controls across identity verification, authentication, session management, transaction monitoring, customer notifications, staff training and incident response. Connect evidence across systems while protecting customer data.

## Key takeaways

- Cyber fraud is a broad category, not a single attack technique.
- An incident may involve several actors, channels and stages.
- Separate what happened from how it happened and what evidence proves.
- Effective prevention combines people, process and technology.
