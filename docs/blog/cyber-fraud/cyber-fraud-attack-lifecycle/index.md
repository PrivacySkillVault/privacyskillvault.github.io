# The Cyber Fraud Attack Lifecycle: From First Contact to Financial Loss

A lifecycle model helps customers, analysts and investigators organise events without assuming every case follows an identical sequence. This article uses a practical cyber-fraud lifecycle. It is an educational model, not a formally standardised framework.

## A practical lifecycle

1. **Target selection:** a person, organisation, account or business process is identified.
2. **Contact and pretext:** the target receives a message, call, advert or social-media approach.
3. **Trust and manipulation:** the criminal builds credibility, creates urgency or exploits a relationship.
4. **Information or access acquisition:** credentials, personal data, payment details or remote access are obtained or abused.
5. **Account or transaction activity:** a login, profile change, beneficiary addition or payment is attempted.
6. **Monetisation and movement:** proceeds may be transferred, withdrawn, converted or passed through intermediaries.
7. **Concealment or persistence:** criminals may maintain access, delete messages or create layers between themselves and proceeds.
8. **Detection and response:** the victim, bank, platform or law-enforcement agency identifies suspicious activity.

Stages can overlap, repeat or occur in a different order. Some attempts are stopped before money moves. Others involve an apparently legitimate customer action induced by deception rather than technical account compromise.

## Do not confuse this with the Cyber Kill Chain

The Lockheed Martin Cyber Kill Chain is a separate seven-stage model for cyber intrusion: reconnaissance, weaponisation, delivery, exploitation, installation, command and control, and actions on objectives. It is useful for analysing intrusion activity, but it is not a universal financial-fraud lifecycle.

MITRE ATT&CK documents adversary tactics and techniques observed in real-world activity. It is not a linear checklist that every incident must follow.

## Applying the lifecycle

For each stage, ask what event is alleged, which system can provide evidence, what timestamp and time zone were recorded, whether the record is direct evidence or inference, and what alternative explanation remains possible.

A useful timeline may join customer contact, authentication, session creation, device enrolment, account changes, beneficiary activity, transaction decisions and downstream payment records.

## Key takeaways

- A lifecycle organises evidence; it does not claim all fraud is identical.
- Financial fraud can involve social engineering, technical compromise or both.
- Keep the fraud lifecycle distinct from the Cyber Kill Chain.
- Record uncertainty and test alternative explanations.
